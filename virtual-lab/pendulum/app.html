Physics Equations

Single Pendulum:

Equation of motion:
d²θ/dt² = -(g/L) × sin(θ) - γ × dθ/dt

Where θ is angle, g is gravity (9.81 m/s²), L is length, and γ is damping coefficient. This is the exact nonlinear equation without small-angle approximation.

Damping Effects

Damping Coefficient Impact:

• Low values (0.90-0.95): High air resistance, rapid energy loss, oscillations decay quickly (like underwater or large surface area pendulum)

• Medium values (0.96-0.98): Normal air resistance, gradual energy loss, realistic laboratory conditions

• High values (0.99-1.00): Minimal resistance, very slow energy loss, near-vacuum conditions (1.00 = no damping)
