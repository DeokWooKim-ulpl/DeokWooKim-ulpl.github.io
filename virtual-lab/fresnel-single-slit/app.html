Theory: From Helmholtz equation to Fresnel diffraction

1. Starting Point - Helmholtz Equation:
For monochromatic waves with time dependence e^(-iωt), the wave equation reduces to the Helmholtz equation:
∇²U + k²U = 0, where k = 2π/λ is the wave number.

2. Fresnel-Kirchhoff Diffraction Theory:
Using Green's theorem and applying boundary conditions at the aperture, the field at observation point P(x,z) is given by:
U(P) = -(i/λ) ∬ U(Q) × [e^(ikr)/r] × [1/2(1 + cos θ)] dS
where r is the distance from aperture point Q to observation point P, and θ is the angle between the normal and r.

3. Fresnel Approximations:
For small diffraction angles (paraxial approximation):
• Distance approximation: r ≈ z + (x-x')²/(2z)
• Angle factor: (1 + cos θ)/2 ≈ 1
• Amplitude term: 1/r ≈ 1/z

4. Fresnel Integral Formulation:
Substituting the approximations into the diffraction integral:
U(x,z) = (C/z) ∫[aperture] U₀(x') × exp[ik(x-x')²/(2z)] dx'
where C is a normalization constant and U₀(x') describes the aperture function.

5. Single Slit Case:
For a single slit of width 'a' with uniform illumination:
• Aperture function: U₀(x') = rect(x'/a) (constant within slit, zero outside)
• Integration limits: x' ∈ [-a/2, +a/2]
• Final form: U(x,z) = (C/z) ∫[-a/2 to +a/2] exp[ik(x-x')²/(2z)] dx'

6. Connection to Fresnel Integrals:
The integral can be expressed in terms of Fresnel integrals C(u) and S(u):
• Fresnel parameters: u₁,₂ = (x ± a/2)√(2/(λz))
• Fresnel integrals: C(u) = ∫[0 to u] cos(πt²/2) dt, S(u) = ∫[0 to u] sin(πt²/2) dt
• Amplitude: U ∝ [C(u₂) - C(u₁)] + i[S(u₂) - S(u₁)]

7. Physical Regimes:
The behavior depends on the Fresnel number F = a²/(2λz):
• F >> 1: Fresnel diffraction regime - oscillatory fringes, geometric shadow effects
• F << 1: Fraunhofer diffraction regime - smooth sinc² pattern
• F ≈ 1: Intermediate regime - transition between the two

8. Intensity Calculation:
The observed intensity is the square magnitude of the complex amplitude:
I(x,z) = |U(x,z)|² = [Re(U)]² + [Im(U)]²

This calculator performs direct numerical integration of the Fresnel diffraction integral, allowing visualization of the transition from Fresnel to Fraunhofer regimes as the observation distance changes.
