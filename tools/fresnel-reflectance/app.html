Physical Principles & Mathematical Formulation

Fresnel Equations

The reflectance at the interface between two dielectric media is governed by the Fresnel equations, which depend on the polarization of incident light.
TE Polarization (s-polarization):
RTE = |rs|² = |(n₁cosθ₁ - n₂cosθ₂) / (n₁cosθ₁ + n₂cosθ₂)|²
TM Polarization (p-polarization):
RTM = |rp|² = |(n₂cosθ₁ - n₁cosθ₂) / (n₂cosθ₁ + n₁cosθ₂)|²
where θ₁ is the incident angle, θ₂ is the refracted angle, and n₁, n₂ are the refractive indices of the two media.

Snell's Law

The relationship between incident and refracted angles is described by Snell's law:
n₁ sin θ₁ = n₂ sin θ₂

Special Angles

Brewster Angle (θB): The angle at which TM-polarized light experiences zero reflection.
θB = arctan(n₂/n₁)
Critical Angle (θc): The angle beyond which total internal reflection occurs (when n₁ > n₂).
θc = arcsin(n₂/n₁)

Key Physical Phenomena

  • Total Internal Reflection: When θ₁ > θc, all incident light is reflected (R = 1)
  • Brewster Effect: At θB, TM-polarized light has zero reflectance
  • Polarization Dependence: TE and TM polarizations exhibit different reflectance behaviors
  • Normal Incidence: At θ₁ = 0°, R = |(n₁-n₂)/(n₁+n₂)|² for both polarizations
