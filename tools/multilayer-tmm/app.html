Theory: Transfer Matrix Method

The Transfer Matrix Method (TMM) calculates optical properties of multilayer systems by relating electric and magnetic field amplitudes using 2×2 matrices. This approach enables precise computation of reflectance and transmittance for stratified media with multiple layers.

Mathematical Formulation

For each layer, the characteristic matrix relates field amplitudes:

C_i = [cos(δ_i) i*sin(δ_i)/u_i] [i*u_i*sin(δ_i) cos(δ_i) ]

Where the phase thickness is: δ_i = (2π/λ) * n_i * d_i * cos(θ_i)

The optical admittance depends on polarization:

  • TE (s-polarized): u_i = n_i * cos(θ_i)
  • TM (p-polarized): u_i = n_i / cos(θ_i)

Reflection and Transmission

The total transfer matrix for the multilayer system: M = C_1 * C_2 * ... * C_L

From which the reflection and transmission coefficients are obtained:

r = (u_0*M_11 - M_21) / (u_0*M_11 + M_21) t = 2*u_0 / (u_0*M_11 + M_21)

Finally, the measurable quantities are:

Reflectance: R = |r|² Transmittance: T = (u_substrate/u_incident) * |t|²

This method properly handles complex refractive indices and evanescent waves when total internal reflection occurs. The calculator is suitable for analyzing antireflection coatings, optical filters, Bragg reflectors, and multilayer systems.

For more information, see O.S. Heavens, "Optical Properties of Thin Films," Reports on Progress in Physics, vol. 23, pp. 1-65, 1960.
